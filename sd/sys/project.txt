GANTRY — MELLOW EVO SPAWD, 9mm -> 12mm BELT CONVERSION
=======================================================================================================

Hardware: Mellow CNC EvoHevORT SPAWD mounts (aluminium replacement for
MirageC's printed HD12 SPAWD parts). Shipped in 9mm belt configuration
("基础版" / base version on all five screw packs). Conversion to 12mm is
a pulley swap PLUS a full re-shim, not shims alone.

Screw pack barcodes (Mellow):
  35010500011  Right front motor bracket
  35010500012  Right rear motor bracket
  35010500013  Left front motor bracket
  35010500014  Left rear motor bracket
  35010500015  Crossbeam left & right pulley bracket

Pulley count per tower (confirmed against MirageC BOM_XY_HD12_SPAWD.xlsx,
items 9/10/11 — 4 of each across four towers):
  1x 12mm toothed motor pulley
  1x 12mm toothed idler
  1x 12mm smooth (toothless) idler
Pulleys and idlers NOT supplied with the Mellow kit — sourced separately,
IN HAND. All shim (precision adjustment gasket) combinations ARE supplied.

Placeholder note: Mellow ship a PRINTED 20mm spacer on the motor shaft in
place of the pulley. It has no groove, so it is not a belt-plane datum —
it only preserves the bore stack height. Do not try to derive belt plane
position from it.

Governing principle: bore stack height is FIXED per position. Shim above +
pulley length + shim below = constant. 12mm pulleys measure 22mm vs the
20mm placeholder, so 2mm of shim comes out per position.

CHAMBER INSULATION
=======================================================================================================

Material    : 25 mm PIR foam board (lambda 0.023 W/m·K), foil-faced
Application : Bonded to INNER face of aluminium panels (chamber-facing).
              Foil facer faces chamber air directly. Dot-and-dab with Dowsil 799.
              Joints taped with standard acrylic HVAC-grade foil tape (Bond It
              75mm x 45m) — standard grade confirmed adequate, chamber runs cooler
              than a boiler flue.
Insulated   : Left side, right side, rear, top, bottom, and the PIR
              portion of the front door
Glazed      : Front door vision panel only

Panel stack:
  Sides / elec bay  5 mm aluminium + 25 mm PIR
  Rear / top        3 mm aluminium + 25 mm PIR
  Bottom            25 mm PIR
  Door (glazed)     6 mm toughened glass, single pane, 469 x 606 mm

GLAZING (RESOLVED 03/08/2026):
  6mm toughened glass, 469 x 606mm, captive on all four sides in the 3030
  slot (8mm wide x 9mm deep). 6mm engagement per edge; 3mm of sealant at
  slot bottom so glass never bottoms hard on aluminium; ~1mm per face in
  the 8mm slot width also filled.
  Sealant: Dowsil 799 CLEAR, IN HAND. Neutral cure — acetoxy would trap
  acetic acid in a gas-tight 1mm gap against bare aluminium with no
  airflow. -40C to +149C, low modulus, primerless to glass and aluminium.
  Clean slot and glass edge with IPA — TDS states detergent/water NOT
  acceptable.
  ASSEMBLY: pane is captive all round, so the door frame must be built
  AROUND the glass — three sides bolted, glass slid in, fourth member
  last. Replacement means dismantling the frame.

  Sealed double-glazed unit REJECTED: supplier confirmed units are not
  pressure-filled and the edge is simply taped — seal fails at sustained
  chamber temperature. Failure mode is gas migration then permanent
  internal condensation, which blinds the window and defeats the purpose.
  Polycarbonate REJECTED as primary pane: 6mm solid PC (~4.0 W/m²K vs
  glass ~5.7) saves only ~30W at the cost of permanent scratching,
  crazing from chamber outgassing, 7x thermal expansion, and creep at
  temperature. Twin-wall PC insulates better but is translucent.

  UPGRADE PATH (not ordered): if commissioning shows glazing loss matters,
  add a PC sheet to the door's OUTER face on 10-15mm standoffs, cavity
  VENTED top and bottom (not sealed). Outer face only — stays cooler, not
  exposed to outgassing, trivially removable. Keep the door frame's outer
  face clean and unobstructed to allow this.

Sealing
  Fixed panels : Silicone RTV applied during installation
  Front door   : 10x3 mm silicone foam tape on door frame face, OR silicone
                 tube cord (10mm OD) in T-slot as an alternative — both
                 ordered for direct comparison once the door frame is built.
                 Green silicone tube received in two bores (OD10xID6 and
                 OD10xID8, 3m each) to test compression fit in the slot;
                 final choice made on the bench, not on paper.
  Panel joints : 10x3 mm silicone foam tape between panels
  Note: all seals silicone-based — handles 60C+ chamber temp

Thermal estimate (60C chamber, 20C ambient, dT = 40C):

  Total estimated chamber heat loss ~108 W

=======================================================================================================
SAFETY CIRCUIT — Duet -> PLC (R1)
=======================================================================================================

Duet OUT8 drives R1 coil directly. HIGH = relay energised = system healthy.
Drops on any Duet stop/estop.

- R1 NO contact -> PLC %I0.0 (DI_EStop_PLC) — 24V when relay energised
- No SR latch in PLC — %I0.0 used directly in FC4. Duet OUT8 is the only latch.
- Duet OUT8 asserted HIGH in config.g at startup.
- Drops via M42 P0 S0 in sleep.g, trigger0.g, trigger3.g.
- OUT8 does NOT drop on M0 (end of print) — only on genuine stop/estop.

=======================================================================================================
MODBUS RTU — Duet <-> PLC
=======================================================================================================

Physical layer : Duet 3 6HC onboard RS485 transceiver. RS485_EN jumper fitted.
                 IO1 dedicated — do not use IO1 for anything else.
Cable          : OSP1 1x2x24AWG overall screened, drain grounded at PLC end only,
                 260mm run — no termination resistor needed
Duet config    : M575 P2 B9600 S7
Protocol       : 9600 8N1, Duet = master, PLC CB1241 = slave address 1
Polling        : daemon.g reads all 5 registers every 5s via M261.1 F3 R0 B5
                 Read implemented via sub-macro sys/plc_read.g (var scope workaround
                 for RRF 3.6.x M261.1 — V parameter creates new var only, never
                 overwrites. Sub-macro scope destroys var on return.)
Note           : RRF 3.6.x has NO bitwise operators — use mod arithmetic for bit extraction
WARNING        : RRF 3.7.0 changes M575 port numbering — P2 becomes P3 for first
                 serial port. Do not upgrade to 3.7.x without updating M575 in config.g.

Register Map (DB1, HR40001 base):
  R0  Chamber_SP        Duet->PLC  Int, tenths degC
  R1  Duet_Heartbeat    Duet->PLC  Int, 0-32767
  R2  Duet_Control_Bits Duet->PLC  UNUSED (was Bit0 = Printer_Active; elec bay
      cooling moved to Duet, bit no longer written or read. DB member retained —
      removing it would shift R3/R4 and break the Modbus map.)
  R3  Chamber_PV        PLC->Duet  Int, tenths degC
  R4  Status_Bits       PLC->Duet  See below

Status_Bits (R4):
  Bit 0  Chamber sensor fault
  Bit 1  Top sensor fault
  Bit 2  Bottom sensor fault
  Bit 3  Heater fault
  Bit 4  PLC heartbeat toggle
  Bit 5  PLC running
  Bit 6  Duet comms OK
  Bit 7  Duet comms fault

  FUSE RATING REVISED 06/08/2026 (supersedes 3A): retain the 5A fuses as
  supplied. Reasoning:
  - Each M23CL carries its own internal V_IN fuse (0157002.DRT, 2A
    Slo-Blo, replaceable from v1.2). That is the device protection. A
    genuine motor fault clears internally before a board fuse reacts.
  - The TDB fuse protects the cable and the JST VH output, which is
    itself rated 5A. Fusing below the item being protected gains nothing.
  - 3A sits too close to real draw. Quiescent per motor at 48V is ~0.16A
    (2 x (2.8/sqrt2)^2 x 0.9 = ~7W copper, /48V, ~90% driver eff), but
    accelerating AWD load adds mechanical work — ~0.8-1A each, higher on
    transients. A 3A blade fuse derates above ~70% of rating in a warm
    bay; nuisance blows were being designed in.
  - 4 x 5A = 20A matches the TDB input connector rating exactly.
  DO NOT fit 3A. Revisit only if motor cabling is found to be under
  5A-rated.

  TDB is passive — no MCU, no firmware, nothing to configure or commission.
  Only per-output LEDs (one per output plus input) and a jumper-selectable
  CAN termination resistor. On install, the only decision is whether the
  termination jumper stays fitted — remove it if anything hangs off the
  RJ11 CAN OUT downstream.
